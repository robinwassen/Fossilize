## Problem

A truncated archive with no retained entries could look like a complete empty database. Missing inputs and replay selection policies could also hide which work was actually covered.

## What changes

- Add owned preparation reports for database inputs, discovery state, retained boundaries, known keys and exclusions, preserving unknown unread totals.
- Share filter, range, hash and creation-hint accounting across replay paths, and check required discovery before empty-work success.
- Add explicit `--accept-recovered-prefix` and `--optional-database-index` policies, forwarded through ExternalReplayer and same-commit OS metadata.
- Check text-archive position arithmetic, guard empty marker writes, and classify unknown directory entries without admitting sockets or following symlinks.

## Why this matters

Callers can distinguish complete empty input from incomplete discovery, choose a recovered prefix explicitly, and see policy exclusions without counting them as successful or failed execution.

## Why this approach

Discovery, selection and execution remain separate facts. Additive nonvirtual APIs preserve existing preparation signatures and tolerant library/capture behavior; owned snapshots retain input identities after failed children are discarded. Shared selection and checked metadata carry those facts into production replay. The existing Options and shared-progress layouts are preserved.
